If you're worried about a friend

You don't need special training to help. You need to take it seriously and not carry it alone.

1

Ask directly. "Are you thinking about suicide?" doesn't put the idea in their head. Asking clearly is what the research actually supports, and it tells them you can handle hearing the real answer.

2

Listen without trying to fix it immediately. You don't need the right words. You need to not look away.

3

Don't promise secrecy. "I won't tell anyone" can feel caring in the moment and be the wrong promise to keep. Tell them you care too much to keep this one to yourself.

4

Bring in a trusted adult: a parent, school counselor, or another adult you both trust. You're allowed to ask for help with this; it's not betrayal.

5

If it feels urgent, don't wait. Call or text 988 together, or call 911 if there's immediate danger.

Warning signs worth knowing

  • Talking about wanting to die, or feeling like a burden to others
  • Feeling hopeless, trapped, or like there's no way out
  • Withdrawing from friends, family, or things they used to care about
  • Giving away belongings, or talking as if saying goodbye
  • Big, sudden shifts in mood, including seeming unusually calm after a low period
  • Increased use of alcohol or drugs, or noticeably reckless behavior

One sign alone isn't a diagnosis. Several together, or a gut feeling something's wrong, is enough reason to ask directly and involve someone else.
