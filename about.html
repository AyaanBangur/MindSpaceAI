About mindspace

Support that's honest about its own limits.

mindspace exists for the moments before a crisis — the ordinary, hard days that don't feel urgent enough to call anyone about, but are heavy enough to sit with alone. We built it to lower the bar for reaching out, not to replace the people and professionals who can actually help when it matters most.

Where this started

mindspace began as a hackathon project built under the theme "when medicine meets CS" — a small team's attempt to use technology for something that mattered more than a demo. What started as a weekend build is now being developed further as a longer-term project, with the same core rule we started with: never pretend to be more than what we are.

What this is — and isn't

What it is

A free, anonymous, judgment-free space to put feelings into words. A calm first step, especially at 2am when nobody else is awake. A bridge toward real support — 988, a counselor, a trusted adult — not a substitute for any of them.

What it isn't

Not therapy. Not a diagnosis. Not a crisis hotline replacement. Not a friend standing in for the people in your life — it will say so itself if a conversation starts to lean that way. If you're in danger, it will always point you to 988 or 911 first.

How we think about safety

Every conversation is screened for signs that someone may be at real risk — not to police what people say, but so the response can shift immediately toward 988, 911, or another real person, instead of continuing as a normal chat. That logic is reviewed by people with relevant background before it's trusted with real conversations, and it keeps improving after launch, not just before it.

We'd rather be a narrower, safer tool than a broad, exciting one. Features that don't clearly serve that get cut before ones that do.

Privacy

  • No account or login required to talk.
  • Conversations aren't tied to a name or identity.
  • Nothing is sold, shared, or used for advertising — there isn't any advertising here.

Who's behind it

mindspace is built by a student, not a clinical institution — that's a deliberate thing to be upfront about. We're actively looking to partner with campus counseling centers and mental health professionals so this tool sits alongside real care rather than trying to stand in for it. If that's you, we'd genuinely like to hear from you.
